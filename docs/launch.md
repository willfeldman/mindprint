## Detailed announcement

We've made tools and workflows portable across AI. What about the perspective of the person using them?

I'm proposing MINDPRINT: an open format for representing how someone approaches decisions, tradeoffs, and communication.

The artifact is one readable file: MINDPRINT.md.

Give your reviewed profile to a coworker. They can ask their AI to review a plan through your lens. Move to another assistant. Bring your working preferences with you.

The builder uses authorized chat history, selected examples, or an interview. It extracts conditional rules and exceptions, removes private source details, and helps you test and review the result. The source conversations stay out of the export.

MINDPRINT is a working model, not a clone or a way to approve things in someone's name. Preferences can be personal information; the owner still needs to review what they share.

The v0.1 proposal includes a specification, producer/consumer rules, a builder skill, a plain-text starter, reference validation and packaging tools, and synthetic examples. SKILL.md is an adapter; MINDPRINT.md is the portable document.

This is the beginning of a standard proposal, not a claim of industry adoption. I'd love feedback from people building agents, personalization, and collaboration tools.

## Thread

1. MCP connects tools. Skills package capabilities. MINDPRINT carries perspective. I'm proposing an open format for the way a person approaches decisions and communication.

2. The file is MINDPRINT.md. Plain Markdown. Human-readable. No special app required to read it. A skill adapter makes it usable in existing Agent Skills workflows.

3. The evidence lives in your corrections: what you push back on, which assumptions you ask to test, when you want more detail, and when you want someone to make the thing. The builder turns those patterns into conditional rules, with exceptions.

4. You can create one from history your assistant can actually access, examples you choose to share, or an interview. It removes private source details, then asks you to review the result. Your raw conversations aren't part of the export.

5. Share the reviewed profile with a coworker or friend. Their AI can use your perspective on a task. It can't claim you approved the answer or make decisions in your name.

6. The proposal ships with a spec, builder, example, and reference tools. Different models may behave differently, and privacy requires review. This is a starting point for interoperability, not a claim that everyone already supports it.

7. Try it. Correct it. Build a consumer. Propose a better rule. The standard should improve through real use: https://github.com/willfeldman/mindprint
