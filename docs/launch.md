## Short post

Your AI has seen you make decisions, reject drafts, and explain what matters.

What if you could turn those patterns into a file a coworker or friend could use?

I built Mindprint: an open-source skill that helps create a portable model of your working style, with private source details removed and a review before sharing.

Your way of thinking. A file you can share.

https://github.com/willfeldman/mindprint

## Thread

1. I asked AI to look at our back-and-forth and figure out how I approach problems. The interesting evidence was in my corrections: what I pushed back on, what I asked it to prove, and when I wanted it to stop analyzing and make the thing.

2. That became a small set of instructions another AI could use. Specific rules, with exceptions. “When the decision is reversible, try a small experiment” is more useful than “I'm analytical.”

3. I turned the process into Mindprint. It can use history your assistant actually has, selected examples, or an interview. It drafts a working-style skill, removes private context, and helps you check whether the result feels right.

4. Then you can give the reviewed file to a coworker or friend: “Use this as a lens when reviewing my plan.” It doesn't speak for you, and it doesn't contain your raw conversations. You still review the output for anything you wouldn't want to share.

5. It's plain Markdown, with a SKILL.md version for agents that support skills. No Mindprint account or model API needed. Different models can behave differently; this is a working model, not a clone.

6. Try it on yourself. The part you disagree with may be the most useful part to correct. Open source: https://github.com/willfeldman/mindprint
